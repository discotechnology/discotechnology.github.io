Computer networking is important because it enables communication and data exchange between computers and devices, forming the backbone of modern information systems. Through networks—whether local area networks (LANs) or wide area networks (WANs)—organizations can share resources such as files, software, and hardware efficiently. This interconnectivity improves collaboration among users, enhances productivity, and allows for centralized management of data, which is essential for both businesses and institutions. Without networking, the seamless sharing of information and resources that we depend on today would be impossible.

Furthermore, networking is crucial for global connectivity and access to information. The Internet, the largest computer network in the world, allows people to communicate instantly, access vast amounts of knowledge, and conduct commerce, education, and entertainment activities online. Networking technologies also support cloud computing, remote work, and the Internet of Things (IoT), where countless devices communicate autonomously. In essence, computer networking not only drives innovation but also underpins nearly every aspect of modern life, making it an indispensable part of our digital world.
